None of these options depends on Macks, Fletcher or Kyle Wi-Fi.

## Path and mount sensitivity

The viewer plots native terrain, a straight antenna ray and the inner 60% of the first Fresnel zone. Either endpoint can be varied. At the southeast bridge, a 2 m tree mount and 0.5 m Lee Fire antenna still clear terrain by 0.66 m. At the longer-carry south bridge the same low mounts leave approximately zero clearance. At nominal 4 m / 1 m, the paths have 1.16 m and 1.43 m respectively. At 6 m / 2 m they rise to 2.16 m and 2.43 m. The numeric height sweep covers 0.5–6 m at stations and 2–8 m at tree sites.

Horizontal siting can dominate mount height. Upper Lee south ridge to Lee Fire shows +1.47 m at the chosen point and **−16.04 m** only 20 m south. Survey that face of the ridge before assigning a case. Several terrain-clear paths have only metres of clearance near an endpoint. Inspect the actual mount, canopy, structures, snow, orientation and winter solar exposure.

## Evidence and reproducibility

The fresh search starts with deployed coordinates, raw OSM geometry, the cached USGS mosaic and native tiles. It uses no earlier candidate rankings, path scores or reachability masks. It screened 6,703 general search positions, 11,054 local positions around Lee Junction and 15 intermediate Fletcher approach positions. A 160 m general grid with local offsets and a 35 m junction grid produce a finite shortlist. A beam search compares hardware-constrained layouts. These are strong tested configurations within the shortlist, not proof of a global optimum.

Terrain rays use 910.625 MHz, a 4/3 effective Earth radius, 4 m tree mounts, 1 m stations and a 1.2 m member antenna. Rough screening samples terrain at 20 m along the ray. Detailed member footprints use the 5 m terrain mosaic, 40 m road segments and 90 m map cells. Backbone profiles sample the native USGS tiles at 1 m intervals with mosaic fallback. The strict check requires at least 60% first-Fresnel clearance on each backbone edge; member footprints use bare-earth visibility. No canopy-loss or radio link-budget calibration was fitted. Ratings exclude temporary valley gateways and unverified Kyle Wi-Fi. The DEM cannot predict the strong observed Shady receptions through nominal obstruction. [USGS 3DEP specifications](https://www.usgs.gov/3d-elevation-program-standards-and-specifications), [OpenStreetMap data attribution](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright)

From the primary checkout containing the cached GIS bundle, run the four Python entry points in order: `model.py`, `layouts.py`, `atlas.py`, `profiles.py` in [scripts/mountain-siting](../../scripts/mountain-siting/). Use the host Python and the pinned GIS dependencies in [the terrain workflow](../../scripts/winter-siting/README.md). Outputs go to local `private/mountain-siting-final-20261117/`. The PDF, GPX and offline HTML share geographic waypoint names. The CSVs include all candidate-pair highway overlaps, native profile margins where calculated, station routes, mount offsets, height sweeps and the individual member probes. A blank native margin means a severely blocked coarse candidate pair was screened out. The elevation grid, proposed survey pins and trial tables remain in the local field packet.

For acceptance, collect direct bidirectional packet IDs per intended hop and ordinary member GPS receipts at Lee Fire. Record actual heights, device and gateway uptime, sent/received counts, GPS fix time and receipt age. Check the same previously working route before and after a move, including on foot at Lee Junction. Gaps remain field questions until repeated deliveries keep member fixes inside the project's 15-minute freshness boundary.
