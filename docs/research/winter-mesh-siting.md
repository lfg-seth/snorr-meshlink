# Winter mesh siting study

The November 2026 planning run compared two station gateways, the owner's seven starting repeater placements and nine-radio alternatives for passive GPS tracking. Coordinate-bearing inputs, navigation, terrain, maps, site sheets and results are kept together under ignored `private/winter-siting/`. Use the [local workflow](../../scripts/winter-siting/README.md) with that private data package.

For the recommended nine, the terrain graph reaches 43.82 of 58.95 km of scored roads, 91.00 of 155.60 km of named trails and 112.97 of 265.40 km² of land. A different ninth placement adds 0.36 km of intact road but reduces road length surviving all single-node removals from 6.50 to 1.36 km. Primary planning retains the two stations. The optional valley experiment has no verified home or radio link.

The reproducible comparison uses a 5 m projected terrain grid for member visibility, native 1 m terrain sampling for backbone profiles, the configured three-forwarder limit, and independent removal of each station or repeater. Aggregate numbers describe modeled terrain connectivity. Uncertain mounting surfaces, seasonal access, body and vehicle loss, vegetation, site approval, local receiving performance and equipment endurance require field evidence.

The private field report carries the site sequence, alternatives, gaps and outage impacts. Start with the Lee gateway and first two relay tests. The highest-impact Kyle-side branch depends on a maintainable upper mounting point and end-to-end receipts through it. Keep physical assignments in the deployment roster when the owner selects hardware.
