## Portable tester authorization — 2026-10-13

Owner confirms RAK4631 cores and Dell-driven testing with a GUI/readout. Exact frequency variants and unit condition remain unverified. Owner accepts a transferable private key on the V4: native PKI with a separate survey public-key allowlist is selected for the first version. Responder is always available to authorized survey pings because placed nodes will be hard to reach; no physical visit or per-survey enable step is required. Dell controls the test sequence; RAK does not initiate survey pings. No key provisioning or implementation performed.

Pinned [Router source](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/blob/54e0d8d0ab2ff56b3a9ce967e53f79e49af560fb/src/mesh/Router.cpp) permits native PKI on private application ports and attaches the sender public key after successful decryption. Recommend a survey-specific public-key allowlist, requiring PKI; do not grant remote-admin rights. The private key lives on the V4. Restoring its keypair to a replacement tester preserves authorization, but both peers need current node-info/key discovery; use one active tester for that credential. Native PKI uses a separate encryption envelope rather than private-channel PSK encryption. Session/sequence duplicate handling and direct-RF/MQTT checks remain required.

A Dell-only signing key was considered as an alternative using a separate signature scheme such as [Ed25519](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8032), with added payload overhead, verification and replay design. It is not selected for the first version. Including a public key or identifier alone in the payload does not prove possession. No authentication implementation has been bench-tested.

Accepted GUI requirements: simple Dell touchscreen interface, large buttons and touch input pads, one-or-more repeater selection and adjustable interval/other test parameters. Numeric settings should have an on-screen number pad; run/site labels need touch text input. GUI is not implemented.

Owner accepts sequential round-robin with one outstanding ping; interval means minimum spacing between individual attempts, not a per-repeater update promise. Timeout/queue delays may increase actual spacing. Controls: large start/pause/stop, adjustable interval/reply timeout and continuous or fixed attempts per repeater. Pause after transport loss and require explicit resume. Automatically save every attempt with optional site/antenna/notes labels and an antenna/configuration-change segment marker. Raw location logs remain private; exact GUI layout/defaults remain open.

Readouts: USB connection status, selectable discovered repeaters with key-readiness status, per-repeater request/response RSSI/SNR, round-trip attempts/successes/timeouts, GPS fix age, host-measured response elapsed time and automatic local log status. Record actual timestamps and available firmware/radio/build configuration alongside user labels.

### Live survey map — accepted scope

Owner requests a live map of logged GPS/signal to revisit dead spots during a run. Display successes and timeouts at the attempt's recorded location; timeouts represent failed round trips, not proven one-way coverage loss. Proposed controls: large map/readout toggle, target-repeater filter, follow-position toggle, touch zoom and touch-selectable past attempts showing outcome, signal readings and GPS age. Keep outcomes visible with symbols/text as well as color. Follow the current GPS position while permitting manual inspection of earlier gaps without stopping the run.

Keep a timestamped GPS stream independent of ping success so movement through a gap remains visible. Do not invent positions between fixes; visibly distinguish estimated connecting lines from recorded GPS samples. Stale/no-fix attempts remain in the log and readout without pretending to locate them precisely. A basemap outage must not stop logging or obscure recorded points/readouts. This is tester scope, not portal/MQTT work. No map or GUI implemented yet.

## Proposed minimal responder contract

1. Ping carries protocol version, test session ID and sequence number, addressed to one repeater. Client records local antenna/configuration and position; no need to transmit the GPS track to the repeater.
2. Repeater returns one short pong echoing session/sequence and its identity, with RSSI (dBm) and SNR (dB) measured on that exact received ping. Missing readings remain missing, never invented as zero. No autonomous survey transmissions or survey log storage on the repeater.
3. V4 attaches its locally measured RSSI/SNR for that exact pong, plus GPS fix time/age/quality and local request/response timing. Measure elapsed time with a monotonic clock; laptop timing includes USB/software latency and is not pure radio propagation time.
4. Both directions must be direct RF. Verify zero-forwarding behavior on the selected firmware for requests AND responses; reject relayed or MQTT-delivered packets. A private channel alone is insufficient proof. Verify isolation with a third-node bench test before calling measurements direct.
5. Bound response rate, ignore malformed/unauthorized requests, suppress duplicate requests, and never respond to pongs. Keep finite duplicate state in RAM and avoid survey flash writes. Agreed access policy: always available to survey-authorized PKI requests, with no per-survey enable step. Bound airtime so surveys do not displace member GPS traffic.
6. Log each attempted ping before waiting, and record timeout, late reply, duplicate, cancellation or transport loss explicitly. A missed pong cannot distinguish a lost ping from a lost response. Round-trip success is measurable; separate directional delivery rates require additional repeater counters/evidence beyond this minimal contract.

This is a proposed contract, not settled packet encoding, port allocation, retry policy, firmware role, or implemented capability. Pin a source revision and board target before building. Preserve the Meshtastic evaluation baseline; a custom responder does not imply a new network protocol.

## Draft repeatable field procedure

1. Bench-check exact radio/core/frequency variants, GPS fixes, metrics, direct-only requests/responses, USB reconnects and persisted attempt/timeout logs. Record firmware revisions and exported configuration without keys. Confirm compliant radio/antenna settings before transmitting.
2. Label each candidate with a site ID. Record temporary access permission, antenna height above ground, orientation, obstruction/terrain notes, mount, panel exposure and weather. Mount a complete representative repeater assembly; temporary test placement does not authorize permanent installation.
3. Keep radio preset/frequency/power, payload size, antenna height/orientation, feedline and test cadence fixed for comparisons. Use one target repeater at a time and verified direct-only settings. Disable retries where supported or explicitly record every attempt/retry; do not hide losses behind eventual delivery.
4. Follow the same representative mission routes and stationary checkpoints. Include known shadowed terrain, canyon bottoms and expected member carrying heights/body orientations. Vehicle/roof antenna measurements form a separate configuration and cannot establish on-foot coverage.
5. Collect repeated attempts at each checkpoint rather than one reading. Proposal: begin with 20 attempts per checkpoint/configuration and increase near coverage edges; this is exploratory sampling, not an agreed pass threshold or reliability guarantee. Choose interval/timeout from selected-preset airtime and queue behavior on the bench, rather than assuming fast ping rates are safe.
6. Compare repeater antennas with the same testing-node setup first; then hold the repeater setup fixed and compare testing-node antennas. Alternate A/B/A where practical to expose changing conditions. Stop transmission before disconnecting antennas. Keep each antenna's feedline/mount recorded so comparisons describe the complete installed configuration.
7. Save successes AND failed attempts with the location recorded at send time. Also record response location when moving; never attach a newer fix silently to an old attempt. Mark stale/no-fix measurements unsuitable for precise coverage mapping. Maintain a separate GPS stream during walking surveys if needed for loss locations.
8. Summarize each site/configuration by route coverage gaps, round-trip success counts/sample size, longest observed gaps, and RSSI/SNR distributions of successful packets. Signal readings exist only for decoded packets, so strong averages can coexist with substantial losses. No universal RSSI cutoff is proposed.
9. Shortlist based on coverage of required member routes plus a usable onward path, solar exposure and service access. Repeat marginal sections under changed conditions before selecting a site. A summit is a candidate, not automatically the best location.
10. Later, separately verify real member GPS traffic through the repeater to the command post/backhaul with representative traffic load, internet outage and repeaters unavailable in turn. Direct-link survey success alone does not establish relay performance or dispatch freshness.

## Primary-source findings and limits

- [RAK19007 datasheet](https://docs.rakwireless.com/product-categories/wisblock/rak19007/datasheet/) specifies nominal 3.7V battery input, 4.2V charging for suitable Li-ion cells, and solar input not exceeding 5.5V. Its sleep figures describe sleep conditions, not measured project repeater operation. [RAK quick start](https://docs.rakwireless.com/product-categories/wisblock/rak19007/quickstart/) warns about battery polarity and specifies 5V solar panels. Actual board revision, cells/controller and cold-charge safeguards remain unverified.
- [Meshtastic Range Test documentation](https://meshtastic.org/docs/configuration/module/range-test/) describes a periodic fixed sender and mobile receiver with GPS-associated logging. It warns about airtime and describes device CSV saving on ESP32 builds. This supports evaluating existing tools, but does not establish the requested on-demand two-direction pong metrics or compatibility with the exact V4/repeater build.
- [Meshtastic packet schema](https://github.com/meshtastic/protobufs/blob/master/meshtastic/mesh.proto) defines receive RSSI/SNR, hop information and MQTT-path metadata. Receive metrics are local metadata, so the proposed pong needs to explicitly carry the repeater's ping measurements. Hop metadata alone has version/presence caveats; verify direct-only behavior against a pinned firmware version.
- [Meshtastic device configuration](https://meshtastic.org/docs/configuration/radio/device/) documents role choices. Verify module behavior and actual relay operation on the selected role/build rather than relying on its name.

No numerical RF settings, legal compliance determination, tested cold-charge capability, antenna superiority, endurance or ideal location is established by this review.

## Dedicated firmware module — follow-up source review

Owner asks for dedicated, addressed, non-hopping survey packets and adjustable usage restrictions. The recommended extension is a compiled C++ module, not a runtime-installed plugin. [Module API](https://meshtastic.org/docs/development/device/module-api/), [SinglePortModule](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/blob/master/src/mesh/SinglePortModule.h) and [module registration](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/blob/master/src/modules/Modules.cpp) provide the extension points. Proposed name: `SignalTestModule`, with `.h/.cpp` files and registration/build inclusion for the selected RAK target. Build/flash custom firmware on responders; no replacement LoRa framing or mesh protocol is needed for this design.

### Packet and direct delivery

- Use a dedicated application port within the upstream private range 256–511; `PRIVATE_APP=256` is available for initial development without regenerating the upstream port enum. Select a project port after checking project collisions. Encode a small versioned binary/protobuf payload with `PING`/`PONG` operation, session ID, sequence, and measurement presence/values. This is a dedicated application packet inside the existing Meshtastic envelope. [Port definitions](https://github.com/meshtastic/protobufs/blob/master/meshtastic/portnums.proto).
- Set destination to the chosen repeater for ping and original testing node for pong, `hop_limit=0`, `want_ack=false`, with application-managed response/timeout. The [range-test sender](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/blob/master/src/modules/RangeTestModule.cpp) already sets zero hops and no ACK. [NextHopRouter](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/blob/master/src/mesh/NextHopRouter.cpp) gates rebroadcast on positive hop limit; its next-hop retry condition also matters, so keep no ACK and zero hops together. Addressing limits processing/delivery, not which neighboring radios can physically hear the signal.
- Explicitly construct/send pongs with zero hops; do not rely on generic reply initialization. [MeshModule](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/blob/master/src/mesh/MeshModule.cpp) applies `setReplyTo`, which sets response hop allowance via [RoutingModule](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/blob/master/src/modules/RoutingModule.cpp). Current routing code has a direct-response branch, but explicit settings avoid dependence on inference/version changes. An application-managed exchange can leave `decoded.want_response=false` and use the PING operation to avoid generic replies/NO_RESPONSE NAKs.
- [Router::sendLocal](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/blob/master/src/mesh/Router.cpp) can replace a user-originated zero-hop setting when ACK is requested. No ACK also avoids [ReliableRouter](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/blob/master/src/mesh/ReliableRouter.cpp) automatic retries. The client controls explicit new attempts, preserving honest loss counts.
- Responder accepts only addressed, authorized, direct RF PINGs and ignores PONGs; include version/path validation and duplicate suppression. Disable MQTT during isolated tests or explicitly exclude the new application port from publishing/ingestion: zero LoRa hops alone does not disable MQTT transport. Verify no relay with a third-node bench test, including a target reachable only through that third node.

### Scope of configurable restrictions

The stock [RangeTestModule](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/blob/master/src/modules/RangeTestModule.cpp) has its own timer, eight-hour cutoff and polite channel-utilization check. A separate module need not inherit those survey policies. Make test cadence/duration and any survey response ceiling configurable; a scoped survey setting could relax the module's own congestion threshold without changing GPS/text traffic policies globally. Laptop-driven scheduling needs no periodic repeater timer.

Lower layers remain separate: [airtime.cpp](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/blob/master/src/airtime.cpp) and [Router::send](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/blob/master/src/mesh/Router.cpp) enforce regional airtime policies and expose `override_duty_cycle`; that is global configuration, not a dedicated-port exemption. The reviewed [region table](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/blob/master/src/mesh/RadioInterface.cpp) uses 100 for US duty-cycle accounting, so the regional-percentage check may not be the survey bottleneck on a US-configured build. This is firmware behavior, not a claim of legal permission for unrestricted transmission. Applicable radio authorization, power, emissions and antenna constraints still need verification; [47 CFR 15.247](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-15/subpart-C/section-15.247) is relevant to some 902–928MHz devices, but the exact core/certification is unknown.

The radio still needs queue capacity and transmit/receive turnaround. [RadioLibInterface](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/blob/master/src/mesh/RadioLibInterface.cpp) waits during active reception/transmission and uses channel activity checks/backoff. Removing those can corrupt survey traffic and does not make the half-duplex radio simultaneous transmit/receive. Log local rejected/queued attempts separately from RF response timeouts; queue acceptance is not proof a packet was transmitted. Distinguishing actual TX time may require extra client-firmware instrumentation.

### Testing node and implementation limits

For a minimal tethered prototype, the Dell can use the [Python client API](https://github.com/meshtastic/python/blob/master/meshtastic/mesh_interface.py) to send dedicated data, receive pongs and local receive metrics, and log GPS supplied by a stock V4. Verify explicit destination/zero-hop/no-ACK handling and local GPS freshness on the exact installed API/firmware; client GPS availability is not assumed from successful radio receipt. An autonomous V4 tester needs its own module/UI/state machine; precise GPS-at-TX and radio timing may also justify client firmware changes. The repeater remains a responder with no survey logger.
